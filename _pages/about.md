I am a PhD candidate in Cognitive Psychology specializing in bilingual episodic memory and discourse processing. My research program examines episodic memory, discourse comprehension, and individual differences, with a primary focus on heritage speakers and linguistically diverse communities. Rather than viewing language processing in isolation, my work examines how cognitive resources, presentation modalities, and cross-language dynamics interact to shape the formation and updating of memory traces over time.

---

## Program of Research

* **How do bilinguals update mental models across modalities?**  
  Comprehension requires continually integrating new or revised information into a coherent mental representation. My work explores how presentation modality (written versus auditory) interacts with language dominance to influence discourse updating.

* **How does cross-language activation reallocate cognitive resources?**  
  My research investigates how non-selective access and cross-language interactions influence episodic memory traces and working memory capacity during complex comprehension tasks.

* **How do individual differences shape real-time cognitive and social behavior?**  
  Bilingualism is fundamentally heterogeneous. I examine how individual variations in heritage language background, proficiency, and language control drive behavioral outcomes, such as real-time lexical alignment in social contexts.

---

## Applied Vision

My goal is to connect micro-level cognitive mechanisms with macro-level discourse processing to better understand bilingual cognition in real-world contexts, informing both theoretical models of memory and practical instructional design for heritage speakers and bilingual learners.
